The experience

What a shopper actually touches: the way in, the welcome, a shopping turn from the shopper's own words to a verified shelf of pieces, and the narrowing chips that take the search deeper. Everything on this page is shown on the live storefront.

How a shopper starts #

The Concierge opens from the Ask JTV chip inside the site search bar. A shopper can type a site search as usual, or tap the chip to hand their words to the Concierge instead. There is no separate app, no login and no install.

The welcome #

The first view offers starter questions as tappable chips. The pool behind them is grounded in aggregate search demand from the storefront's own analytics, and the set is rebuilt on purpose: nine chips per set, never fewer than six, mixed across gift and occasion asks, discovery, brands, budget and service so consecutive sets read differently. Tapping a chip sends the question as if the shopper had typed it.

A shopping turn #

A shopper says what they want in their own words. The agent answers in three moves: an opening line that acknowledges the request, one line that carries what the tiles cannot (how it read the request, what the category runs deep in), and one question that moves the search forward. Beside the text sit the product tiles and the narrowing chips. The tiles carry every name and price; the reply itself names no piece and quotes no count.

The tiles #

Every tile is a real catalog record: image, name and price. Three guarantees sit under the shelf. First, the agent chose these pieces; the search result alone never renders. Second, the application verified each pick against the piece's actual record before it rendered: it exists, it is in stock, it is priced, it is photographed, and it is not a same-design twin of a tile already showing. Third, a full shelf never dips below six tiles when the catalog can fill it: if verification leaves the shelf thin, the application backfills from the same search, and if that is still thin, it runs one more facet-filtered search itself. The shopper sees only pieces that passed, and never a tile the agent described but that did not render.

The narrowing chips #

Under the reply sit the narrowing options the agent chose for this search: the facets that genuinely split the results, shown with their verbatim catalog values, most results first, at most six to a row. The first level needs no label; its values are self-evident. A tap refines the search inside the same turn: the next level's values come from the refinement's own counts, computed within the selection, exactly like guided navigation. A back control returns to the previous level.

Education and policy answers #

Care questions ("how do I clean my gold chain?") are answered from a reviewed blog corpus, in short plain paragraphs, with a link to the article the answer came from. Service questions ("how do returns work?") are answered from a reviewed help corpus and are formatted for a quick scan: the key rule with a short label, the steps as a numbered list, the caveats, and the link. Every mechanic comes from the retrieved page; where the page is silent, the agent says so rather than filling the gap from general knowledge.

The boundaries the shopper feels #

The agent speaks as the store, in the first person, and never hides the system from the shopper in the other direction: it will not pretend to place an order, check a delivery date, read today's TV schedule or guarantee a ring size. It explains what it cannot do and offers the nearest thing it can. If a search comes back thin, it says how few and offers to widen rather than padding the shelf. Prices are USD, and a stated budget is a ceiling, never a target.
