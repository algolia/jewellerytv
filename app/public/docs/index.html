The package

This is documentation for an agentic commerce experience for jewelry retail: a conversational shopping assistant that discovers, verifies and explains real products from a live catalog. It is built on Algolia and shown end to end on a working storefront, so every claim in these chapters can be checked against a running system.

What you are looking at #

A shopper opens the storefront, taps Ask JTV in the search bar, and describes what they want in ordinary words. A conversational agent interprets the request, searches a live product catalog, chooses the pieces worth showing, verifies every one of them, and replies beside a shelf of real product tiles with real prices. The shopper narrows with a tap, asks care and policy questions, and links through to product pages. Nothing in that sentence is scripted: the agent composes every reply, and the application enforces every fact.

The package has four parts:

What the experience does #

  • Conversational discovery. The shopper describes what they want; the agent searches the live catalog and shows a first cut of real pieces, then narrows through conversation rather than forms.
  • Curated, verified shelves. The agent picks the pieces to show; the application re-checks every pick against the actual catalog record (exists, in stock, priced, photographed, not a duplicate design, matches the named material or stone) before anything renders. A piece that fails a check can never appear.
  • Tap-to-narrow chips. After a search, the agent chooses the narrowing options worth showing; each chip tap refines the results in place, one level at a time.
  • Education and policy answers. Care and how-to questions draw on a reviewed blog corpus; returns, shipping and account questions draw on a reviewed help corpus. Both cite their source page.
  • Honest boundaries. The agent never claims an order, a cart change, a delivery date or a live inventory check, and says plainly what it cannot do.

What it deliberately does not do #

The catalog is read-only to this system: no record or index setting can be changed by the agent or the application. There is no cart, checkout, order or account access in the agent's tool set, and the instructions forbid claiming any. When the catalog lacks something the shopper asked for, the agent says so rather than showing something close.
